Efficiency Frontiers

Quantization Lab

INT8/FP8/INT4 workbench with real perplexity degradation on a trained tiny LM

1The problem

Serving an LLM is dominated by moving weights from memory to compute: a 70B model in fp16 needs ~140 GB just to hold the parameters, and every generated token streams those weights through the chip again. Quantization -- storing weights in INT8, FP8 or INT4 -- attacks that bottleneck directly, but every scheme trades bits for model quality, and the granularity of the scheme (per-tensor vs per-channel vs per-group) decides how gracefully it handles the outlier weights real LLMs develop.

2The current gap

Most quantization write-ups either quote vendor numbers or show toy round-trip error on random matrices. What is missing is a closed loop you can run on a laptop: train a real (if tiny) language model from scratch, quantize its actual trained weights with several schemes, and measure the end-to-end effect on language modeling quality -- with every step seeded and reproducible.

3How the solution addresses it

quant_lab is a self-contained quantization workbench in numpy (CPU only):

  • schemes.py -- symmetric per-channel INT8, per-tensor INT8 baseline, per-group symmetric INT4 (group sizes 32/128), and a faithful simulation of the OCP FP8 E4M3 grid (1 sign + 4 exponent + 3 mantissa bits, bias 7, saturating at +/-448, NaN pattern excluded). Each scheme returns dequantized float32 weights plus metadata (scales) and honest bit-rate accounting assuming fp16 scales.
  • metrics.py -- relative L2 reconstruction error, SNR (dB), max-abs and mean-abs error.
  • tiny_lm.py -- a minimal character-level transformer LM in pure numpy (d_model=64, 2 layers, 4 heads, d_ff=128, context 64, ~29-char vocab, ~74k params) with a handwritten forward/backward pass (finite-difference gradient-checked) and an Adam training loop.
  • eval.py -- eval-set perplexity under quantization. Only Linear weight matrices are quantized; embeddings, norms and biases stay fp32 (embeddings are lookups, not matmuls; norms/biases are <1% of params -- the same split production pipelines use).

experiments/run_experiments.py (seeded np.random.default_rng(41)) trains the LM once, then: (1) benchmarks reconstruction error on synthetic weight matrices with LLM-like outlier structure; (2) evaluates perplexity per scheme -- running the full eval sweep twice (weights reloaded from disk) to prove determinism; (3) sweeps per-layer sensitivity with INT4.

6Insights

  1. Granularity is about outliers, not averages. On outlier-structured weights, per-tensor INT8 has ~8x worse relative-L2 error than per-channel INT8 (0.37-0.39 vs 0.047-0.049): one hot channel forces a huge global scale, crushing the many small weights, while max-abs error barely differs. On the tiny trained model -- which developed no strong outlier channels -- per-tensor and per-channel tie at 8 bits. The synthetic experiment isolates the mechanism; the trained-model experiment shows when it does and does not bite.
  2. FP8 has a split personality. The log-spaced E4M3 grid fits the bell-shaped bulk of weights beautifully (best rel-L2 of all schemes at 0.032) but quantizes large magnitudes coarsely (worst max-abs error, 0.50-0.98). As an unscaled direct cast on trained weights it still cost only +0.008 perplexity -- tiny-model weights live inside FP8's comfort zone (2^-9..448). This is also why production FP8 always pairs the format with a scale: without one, any weight below ~2^-9 flushes to zero.
  3. INT4 granularity is a clean rate/quality knob. Shrinking the group 128 -> 32 nearly halved the perplexity penalty (+0.074 -> +0.036) for +0.28 bits/param -- the knee of the Pareto frontier sits at INT4-g32.
  4. Early layers are the sensitive ones. Quantizing only the first transformer block cost +0.029 perplexity; the second block was effectively immune (-0.003, i.e. noise). A mixed-precision recipe would spend its bit budget on early layers first.
  5. INT8 is effectively lossless here (delta < 0.001, within eval noise on a ~1.2k-char eval set) -- the honest claim is the tie, not the ranking between per-tensor and per-channel.
